Four modelling decisions

  • 1Events at the centreA sale is an event, not a line between two people

    A purchase links a seller, a buyer, an object and a time, which a direct relation cannot hold. R6 “sold manuscripts to” becomes a crm:E96_Purchase.

    From theory to formalization
  • 2EvidenceEvery relation says where it comes from

    Direct relations are stated in the frozen article, without implying independent historical verification; Enriched ones (R16–R19) need another source, and each names its sources in the TEI and as dcterms:source in the RDF.

    Evidence types
  • 3◇ What the source does not sayR9 and R12 stay in the theory

    The British Library and the Tokyo National Museum hold manuscripts, but the article does not say which ones, so no holding triple is asserted in the RDF. In the diagram they carry the ◇.

  • 4One documented item · EnrichedThe Diamond Sutra of 868 (R17–R19)

R9 and R12 (◇)

R9 and R12 (held_by: British Library, Tokyo National Museum) are kept as relations of the theoretical model, but no holding triple is asserted for them in the RDF, because the source does not say which manuscripts these institutions hold. The ◇ marks this; the line style still shows only the evidence type.

R17–R19 add one documented item that the page does not name: the printed Diamond Sutra of 868 (British Library, Or.8210/P.2), from the International Dunhuang Programme. R19 (the British Library holds it) is one documented case of R9, not a formalisation of it: R9 stays a general relation, still marked ◇. All three are Enriched, and each points to its own sources.

Wang Yuanlu and Aurel Stein appear in more than one narrative layer, where they act, so that the arrows stay short. A repeated box keeps the same number and refers to the same entity; it is not an additional entity. The theoretical model contains 16 distinct entities.

Subjects, identifiers and evidence

A · Identifiers and authority links. Every authority number is a crm:E42_Identifier with its scheme (crm:E55_Type) and its value (crm:P190_has_symbolic_content). External links are chosen by what they claim: {{models:identity}}

B · Subjects. The six traditions named in the source form a SKOS concept scheme. skos:exactMatch aligns all six with Wikidata items and five with Library of Congress Subject Headings (no heading was found for Nestorian Christianity). It aligns concepts; it does not say that two real-world things are identical, nor that the corpus is Buddhism.

C · Evidence. {{models:statements}} Three kinds of resource are kept apart:

  1. The historical event, as data — purchase-stein (crm:E96_Purchase); the triple purchase-stein crm:P22_transferred_title_to aurel-stein is a fact of the dataset.
  2. The restatement — statement/cr10 (rdf:Statement) restates that one triple so that it can be annotated. It is neither the sale nor the proposition.
  3. The proposition — R6 (crm:E89_Propositional_Object) is a relation of the theoretical model (“sold manuscripts to”). Statements point to it with dcterms:relation; R6 is restated by {{models:r6-count}} statements, R9 and R12 by none.

2.4
Choices and reasons

Modelling decisions

Modelling caseChoiceRationale
Manuscript corpuscrm:E18_Physical_Thing; bought or moved groups as crm:E19_Physical_ObjectThe dispersed corpus is treated as a broad physical thing, not an E78_Curated_Holding, since no single actor curates it; E19 is kept for the identifiable groups of manuscripts.
Discoverycrmsci:S19_Encounter_EventFinding the cave is the first encounter with something that already existed.
Purchasecrm:E96_PurchaseA sale links a seller, a buyer, an object and a time, which a direct relation between two people cannot hold.
Transfer to Beijingcrm:E9_Move of an E19 subsetP25_moved applies to physical objects, and the source says only the remaining Chinese manuscripts were moved.
Digitizationcrmdig:D2_Digitization_ProcessThe digital surrogate is kept separate from the dispersed manuscripts it copies.
Diamond Sutra, part of the corpus (R17)crm:P46i_forms_part_of the corpusA historical physical part-whole relation: the IDP records the scroll among the bundles of Cave 17, and the corpus is the physical aggregate sealed there. In CIDOC CRM 7.1.3, P46 “does not specify when and for how long a component element resided in the respective whole”: the triple does not say that the corpus is still kept together, nor when the scroll left it. It is not a classification; what the texts are about is given by dcterms:subject.
Diamond Sutra, bought by Stein (R18)crm:P24_transferred_title_of from the existing purchase by SteinBased on the IDP's research on Stein's acquisition of 1907 (the bundles handed over between 21 May and 6 June 1907), not on an item-by-item list of the transaction. It records the transfer as these sources describe it, and makes no judgement on the legitimacy of the transaction.
Diamond Sutra, British Library (R19)crm:P50_has_current_keeperCustody, not ownership (ownership would be crm:P52_has_current_owner, which is not used).
