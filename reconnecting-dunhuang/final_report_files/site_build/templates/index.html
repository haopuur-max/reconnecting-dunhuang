01

The problem

One library, sealed in a cave, now scattered across the world

Most of the Dunhuang manuscripts come from a cache sealed in the Library Cave of the Mogao Caves, near Dunhuang. Wang Yuanlu found it in 1900. From 1907 much of the cache was sold to Aurel Stein, Paul Pelliot, Ōtani Kōzui and others, and in 1910 most of the remaining Chinese manuscripts were taken to Beijing.

Today the manuscripts are held in several countries, and the International Dunhuang Project is reuniting them in digital form.

Why this topic

I chose the Dunhuang manuscripts because their history presents a compelling challenge for knowledge representation. Originally preserved together, these materials were later dispersed across different institutions and countries. This project explores how TEI encoding, ontology-based modelling and RDF can make their historical relationships explicit and reconnect information about the dispersed corpus.

  1. ITogetherOriginal contextearly 11th centuryThe manuscripts are sealed in the Library Cave, at the Mogao Caves near Dunhuang, on the Silk Road.
  2. IIDiscoveredDiscovery1900Wang Yuanlu finds the sealed cave and its manuscripts.
  3. IIIDispersedDispersalfrom 1907Manuscripts are sold to Aurel Stein and Paul Pelliot; hundreds more go to Ōtani Kōzui and others (no date in the source).

These five layers organise the theoretical model; the events and their dates are on the Domain page.

Limitations

  1. One frozen source. The relations follow one Wikipedia revision. Only the four Enriched relations draw on other sources, each named: R16 is the project’s interpretation of the International Dunhuang Project’s own description, and R17–R19 come from its catalogue and research. Authority identifiers (Wikidata, VIAF, LC, FAST) are links, not sources of relations.
  2. R9 and R12 stay in the theory (◇). The article says the British Library and the Tokyo National Museum hold manuscripts, but not which ones, so no holding triple is asserted in the RDF.
  3. URIs not yet dereferenceable. The project namespace is fixed, but the site is not yet published at that address; all checks are local.
